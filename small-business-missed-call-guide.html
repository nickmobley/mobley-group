Field Guide · Small business operations · October 2026

Missed calls at your business? Start with what actually happens.

Before you buy an AI receptionist, missed-call text-back service, or booking automation, spend a little time learning where the customer journey breaks. A good workflow fixes a verified problem.

Explore services · For Las Vegas and Henderson businesses

1. Find your real baseline

Look at a representative week of business call logs, website inquiries, and messages, where you have permission to review them. Count incoming contacts, identify unanswered calls, and note when someone returned the call. If your phone system cannot identify legitimate inquiries, review a smaller sample instead of guessing.

Keep the count honest: don't treat every ring as a lost paying customer. Spam, wrong numbers, repeat callers, existing customers, and sales calls may all be mixed into your logs.

2. Map the next step

For a legitimate inquiry, ask: who answers, what questions can they answer, where does the information go, and who follows up? Write the current path on one page. You may discover that your real issue is not missed calls, but an inconsistent handoff after a call is answered.

  • What information does the caller actually need?
  • What questions or appointment requests happen most often?
  • How do you escalate an urgent or complex issue to a person?
  • What business hours and response windows can you reliably support?

3. Choose the smallest useful solution

Human process improvement: a clear callback owner and shared log may solve the problem without new software.

Missed-call text-back: potentially useful when a compatible phone system can trigger a permitted message and route replies. Confirm messaging permissions and customer consent requirements.

AI voice receptionist: may help when callers ask repeatable questions or need an initial message taken. You still need approved scripts, privacy boundaries, clear escalation, and testing.

Website or contact-path repair: sometimes the fastest win is a more visible contact option or fixing a broken booking path.

4. Test before and after

Define a small set of scenarios using sample inquiries. Verify that callers hear or receive the approved response, that information reaches the right destination, that failures are visible, and that a human can take over. Document the results rather than calling the system successful after one happy-path test.

5. Ask about total costs and ownership

Before commissioning a build, ask what the setup costs, which phone, SMS, model, hosting, and integration subscriptions remain in your accounts, who handles maintenance, and what happens if a service is discontinued. Require a written scope and handover instructions.

There is no universal right solution. A small operation with two calls a day has different needs from a busy team fielding inquiries throughout the evening.
