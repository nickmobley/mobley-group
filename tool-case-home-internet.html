Tool Case · Pilot · Henderson / Inspirada · October 2026

“I already have fiber. Would Starlink improve my internet — or should it be my backup?”

This case was built for one homeowner's real question in Inspirada, Henderson, Nevada — including the follow-up every satellite question runs into: “Is the HOA even going to allow a dish?” Researched October 29, 2026 against current sources. One limit, stated plainly: no connection and no mounting location at the home has been tested. This case works from published service information and public rules — the tests below are how the homeowner proves the rest.

The recommendation

Keep reliable fiber as the main connection. Consider a second connection only when the cost and disruption of outages justify it — and first establish whether the problem you're solving is price, outages, weak Wi-Fi, or staying connected away from home. Those are four different decisions wearing one coat.

Why: the fiber already in this house is faster, steadier, and cheaper than satellite on every published measure except one — it depends on a cable in the ground. Starlink's real value here is independence from that cable, priced below as a backup, not a replacement.

1 · The HOA question, answered first

No — the HOA cannot ban the dish. Two layers say so, and both were read at the source:

  • Federal rule (verified)The FCC's OTARD rule (47 C.F.R. § 1.4000) voids HOA restrictions that unreasonably delay or prevent installation, unreasonably raise cost, or block an acceptable signal for dishes one meter or smaller on property under the owner's exclusive use or control. Every Starlink dish is far under the limit — the current Standard is about 23 × 15 inches. The HOA generally cannot require prior approval or a permit fee either; narrow safety and historic-preservation exceptions survive. FCC 19-36 · plain-English summary
  • Inspirada's own rules (verified)The Inspirada Community Association Rules & Regulations ban visible antennas except “Permitted Devices” — dishes one meter or less — which “shall be permitted,” subject to location and screening requirements in the Design Guidelines and possibly City of Henderson approval. Rules & Regulations (PDF)
  • Good terms (guidance)Mount on the back slope of the roof where it still sees open sky, and send the design review committee a short courtesy note with a photo. The law doesn't require their approval — the note costs nothing and prevents a violation letter. Prompt 2 below writes it.

2 · The findings that matter

  • Fiber vs. satelliteQuantum Fiber publishes symmetrical 500 Mbps and ~940 Mbps options for Henderson at roughly $50–75/mo, equipment included. Starlink residential plans run $55 / $85 / $130 per month (100 / 200 / fastest tiers), plus a $349 Standard Kit or a $10/mo kit fee. Real-world Starlink medians: ~130 Mbps down, only 10–30 Mbps up, 25–50 ms latency. Published capabilities support keeping healthy fiber; they don't establish any one home's actual performance.
  • A different routeSatellite removes dependence on the local fiber run. It still needs power, a clear sky view, and can suffer its own congestion, obstruction, and weather interruptions.
  • Standby ModeStarlink offers a $10/mo Standby Mode: the dish stays alive with slow, emergency-level data (up to ~500 Kbps). Treat it as a keep-alive, not a failover — resuming full-speed service is a manual reactivation, and a dual-WAN router cannot resume a paused plan for you.
  • The checkout surpriseStarlink adds a one-time, address-based “demand surcharge” in some areas — reported anywhere from $100 to $1,500 — visible only at checkout with the exact address. Whether it applies at a given Henderson address could not be verified from outside an order. Check before buying hardware anywhere else.
  • HeatThe current dish is rated −22°F to 122°F and sealed (IP67). The famous thermal-shutdown stories were 2021 first-generation hardware. Henderson afternoons sit inside the current rating; mount with airflow underneath and avoid the most heat-soaked roof spot when there's a choice.
  • MountingStarlink wants a wide-open sky (110° field of view). On small lots between two-story homes, ground spots get blocked by rooflines — the back roof slope usually solves it. Run the Starlink app's obstruction scan at the actual planned height and spot before ordering.

4 · Do these first

  • 1Name the one problem you're solving — price, outages, Wi-Fi, or away-from-home.
  • 2Record your current bill, plan, and promotion expiration.
  • 3Run a wired evening speed test; log interruptions separately from Wi-Fi coverage problems.
  • 4Scan proposed dish locations with the Starlink app from safely accessible positions; use a qualified installer for roof access.
  • 5Pull your HOA's current satellite/mounting provisions (Prompt 2 asks for them properly).
  • 6Compare complete first-year costs on the worksheet — including any checkout surcharge.
  • 7List the essentials that must keep working: cameras, work VPN, phones, anything with a job to do.
  • 8If you test a second connection, keep the original service live during the whole evaluation.

6 · Eight prompts for your own AI

Copy any of these into the AI you already use and fill in the [brackets]. Ask for official sources on anything that changes — prices, plans, rules — and keep passwords, account numbers, and payment details out of the conversation.

1 — Make the decision

I live in [area] and use [provider/plan] for [monthly cost]. My problem is [outages / price / Wi-Fi / other]. Compare keeping it, improving my home network, replacing it with Starlink, and adding Starlink as a backup. Ask only for missing details that materially change the answer. Use current official sources for offers and terms, separate published information from assumptions, and leave unverified prices blank. Give me a recommendation, first-year costs with clear inputs, and one next action.

2 — Write the HOA courtesy inquiry

Write a polite email under 150 words to [association name]. I am considering a Starlink dish (23 by 15 inches, under the one-meter limit in the FCC OTARD rule) on [proposed location]. Ask for the current satellite-antenna provisions, any exception to prior architectural approval, and relevant mounting or cable-routing requirements. Do not claim my location already complies or that approval has been granted. Include placeholders for the hardware dimensions and a proposed-location photo — and do not say a photo is attached unless I confirm it.

3 — Plan the obstruction check

Using current official Starlink instructions for [hardware model], explain how to scan two or three safely accessible proposed locations at the intended mounting height. Help me record and compare results for video calls, gaming, and streaming. Explain the uncertainty in the results and how to test for interruptions; do not invent a universally acceptable obstruction percentage. Use the app's orientation guidance rather than assuming a direction. Flag clearly when a professional installer is the right call.

4 — Design and test failover

My primary connection is [provider / equipment]. My proposed backup is [provider / equipment]. My existing router is [model], my budget is [amount], and my essential apps are [list]. Compare automatic failover on an active plan against manual high-speed reactivation from Starlink Standby Mode. Explain power backup, router compatibility, and bypass mode if needed, using current official documentation before recommending any equipment. Give me a reversible setup plan, a primary-disconnection test, and a recovery test.

5 — Troubleshoot slow evenings

Help me investigate slow evening internet on [provider]. Separate Wi-Fi performance from the internet connection itself using wired tests and router/app stats where available. Work through device load, cables, obstructions if relevant, plan limits, outages, and congestion — one useful measurement at a time. Explain what each result establishes and what it leaves unknown before recommending any change or purchase.

6 — Make the Quantum Fiber pricing call

Write a friendly phone script, under five minutes, for calling Quantum Fiber. I currently pay [amount] for [plan]. I like the service and I am not threatening to cancel. Ask about existing-customer offers, when my introductory pricing expires, later prices, equipment charges, payment-method conditions, and whether a different tier costs the same as what I pay now. Ask them to send the complete monthly price and terms in writing. Do not assume published new-customer promotions apply to me.

7 — Prepare a store outage plan

Build a one-page internet continuity plan for a small retail store using [payment system], [shipping software], [phones], and [network equipment]. Start with required vendor/franchise IT compatibility and approved offline procedures. Compare cellular and satellite backup using current official terms and actual address quotes. Separate automatic failover from manual reactivation. Include a first-five-minutes staff checklist, battery power, a test schedule, costs, and unresolved requirements. Do not assume payment or shipping software works at a particular backup speed without testing.

8 — Check occasional travel use

Explain current Starlink options for [road trips / RV / other travel] using official sources. Compare hardware, plans, coverage, data limits, pauses, and reactivation costs for [trip frequency and locations]. Distinguish stationary use, in-motion use, coastal, and open-ocean use. If a cruise is involved, check the named cruise operator's equipment rules before assuming a personal Starlink is permitted on board.

What we could not verify

  • Current HOA guidelinesInspirada's current Design Guidelines placement/screening specifics — the public document is the 2013 Rules & Regulations; the current guidelines sit behind a resident login.
  • Demand surchargeWhether Starlink is charging one at Henderson/Inspirada addresses right now — it appears only at checkout with an exact address.
  • Private groupsAnything inside Inspirada's members-only resident groups. Public Henderson-area posts describe Starlink as underwhelming next to wired options in suburbs; nothing dish-by-dish from inside the neighborhood was publicly readable, and none was invented.
  • Your exact planThe homeowner's precise Quantum Fiber plan and price — comparisons use published Henderson pricing. The worksheet fixes that with real numbers.

Sources consulted October 29, 2026: FCC OTARD materials (FCC 19-36), Inspirada Community Association Rules & Regulations, Starlink plan and support pages, Quantum Fiber Henderson pricing pages, and independent plan trackers (satelliteinternet.com, highspeedinternet.com, budgetseniors.com). Prices and plan terms change; the prompts above are how you re-check them.
