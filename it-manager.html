IT Manager

Your business runs on accounts nobody is watching.

The IT & AI Safety Checkup is a fixed-fee, one-sitting review of the technology a small business actually runs on — email, accounts, devices, backups, AI tools, and the places money moves. You get a written report of what was found and what it risks. Nothing is changed on your systems during the checkup, and we never ask for your passwords.

What gets checked

  • Identity & accessMFA on the accounts that matter, password hygiene, shared logins, and whether people who left still have access.
  • Email & domain trustSPF, DKIM, and DMARC — the records that decide whether a criminal can send email pretending to be your business.
  • Devices & updatesSupported systems, automatic updates, encryption, screen lock, endpoint protection.
  • Backups & recoveryWhat is backed up, whether it's automatic — and whether a restore has ever actually been tested.
  • AI tools & agent safetyWhich AI tools your team uses, what business data goes into them, who owns the accounts, and whether spending caps exist.
  • Money & payout surfacesPayout-change alerts, who can move money, and how payment links get sent.
  • Public surfaceWebsite security and ownership, business listings, and who really owns your social accounts.

How it works

1 · The sitting

Sixty to ninety minutes with the owner, on the owner's own screens. You sign in; we read and ask. Credentials are never requested, held, or written down.

2 · The report

A written findings report: what was checked, what was observed, the risk in plain language, and a fix menu. Anything we could not verify is printed as unverified — never assumed.

3 · The fixes

Each fix is a separate line item you approve or skip. Fixes are done supervised, with you present — never silently, never unattended.

4 · Kept green

Monthly IT Manager coverage re-verifies the checklist on a cycle: starter and leaver access, backup and restore spot-checks, AI-tool watch, and a named human to call before something odd becomes an incident.

Who it's for

Small businesses — roughly 2 to 25 people — with no IT person: owner-operators, professional practices, shops, and studios where IT is whoever has time. If you already have an IT department or a managed-service contract, you don't need this.
