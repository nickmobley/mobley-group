Tool Cases · Pilot

Some decisions deserve more than an opinion.

A Tool Case is a decision toolkit we build for one real question — yours. We research it against current sources, separate what's verified from what's guesswork, and hand you the working tools: a comparison worksheet, step-by-step checklists, a test you can run to prove the answer, and prompts you can reuse with your own AI long after we're done. It sells decision quality, never outcomes — and it says so on every page.

What's inside every Tool Case

  • The findings that matterThe handful of facts that actually decide the question, each with its source linked so you can check it yourself.
  • A worksheetYour real numbers next to the alternatives — first-year costs counted honestly, blanks left blank where a price can't be verified.
  • Do-first checklistsThe short list of steps that come before spending anything, in the order they should happen.
  • An acceptance testA way to prove the decision worked — timed, observable, pass or needs-work. No vibes.
  • Prompts for your own AICopy-paste prompts that hand the research method to whatever AI you already use, so the toolkit keeps working after delivery.
  • What we could not verifyEvery case ends with its unknowns in plain sight. If a document is behind a login or a price only appears at checkout, the case says that instead of guessing.

The working example

Home Internet Tool Case — fiber, Starlink, and a backup plan

Built in October 2026 for a Henderson homeowner's real question: "I already have fiber. Would Starlink improve my internet, or should I use it as backup — and can my HOA even stop me?"

The case answers the HOA question from the FCC rule and the community's own published rules, prices the current plans, compares them honestly against the fiber already in the house, and lands on a recommendation with a worksheet, an outage acceptance test, and eight prompts. No connection at the home was tested — the case says that too.

How a case gets made

1 · You bring the question

A decision you're actually facing — a service to switch, a system to buy, a plan to test. Plain words are fine.

2 · We research and verify

Current sources, rules and prices checked at the source, facts labeled verified or guidance, unknowns kept visible.

3 · You get the toolkit

Findings, worksheet, checklists, acceptance test, and prompts — published as your page and yours to keep and reuse.

4 · You decide

The case recommends; you rule. If the honest answer is "keep what you have," the case says that — the first one did.

Pilot terms

Tool Cases are a pilot service. There is no shelf price yet, on purpose: each case is scoped and priced per case, in writing, before any work starts — what gets researched, what you receive, what it costs. If the scope doesn't earn its price, don't start it. The research standard doesn't change with the price: verified or labeled, sources linked, unknowns named.

A Tool Case is research and decision support. It is not legal, financial, or engineering advice, and it doesn't replace testing at your own home or business — each case tells you what to test.
