Your clients live on their phones. Give them your app.

We design and build a custom app for your business — the one your clients open to book, check in, follow their plan, and check on the dog — built in your own developer accounts and handed over with the keys.

Who it's for — and the problem it solves

Who it's for: owners of appointment- and membership-based businesses — med spas and aesthetics studios, pet care, trainers and fitness studios, and medical and dental practices — whose clients book, pay, follow a program, and check back from their phones.

The problem: your clients already live in apps, while your business reaches them through a mobile website that buries booking behind menus and a social profile you don't control. A client app of your own puts booking, reminders, progress, and your brand on the screen they check all day — in your accounts, under your name.

Four businesses where the need is already proven

These four were not picked by habit. In each one, owners already pay monthly for client-experience software, clients already have something to do in an app between visits, and the platforms they rent leave that experience carrying someone else's name.

FOR MED SPAS & AESTHETICS

Med spa → client app

Your business runs on cycles and series — an app is where your clients live between treatments:

  • Treatment plans with sessions remaining, visible at a glance
  • Pre-care and post-care instructions tied to each treatment
  • A before-and-after photo timeline your client keeps
  • Rebooking reminders timed to the treatment cycle, not the calendar
  • Membership status, perks, and regimen checklists
FOR PET CARE

Grooming, boarding & daycare → pet parent app

Your clients are pet parents, and between drop-off and pickup they want one thing — to see how their dog is doing:

  • Photo report cards after every visit or day of play
  • Booking for grooming, boarding, and daycare in a few taps
  • Vaccination records uploaded once, with expiry reminders
  • Feeding and medication instructions kept on file
  • Package balances and easy rebooking
FOR TRAINERS & STUDIOS

Trainer or studio → member app

Your coaching and your classes, under your brand instead of a platform's:

  • Training programs your clients follow in the app
  • Class and session booking with pack balances
  • Habit and session check-ins you can see at a glance
  • Progress tracking — measurements, milestones, streaks
  • Messaging with stated boundaries: your hours, your response windows
FOR PRACTICES & DENTISTS

Practice or dental office → patient app

The paperwork and the follow-through, handled before and between visits:

  • Intake and consent forms completed before arrival
  • Appointment check-ins, reminders, and recare recalls
  • Visit prep and post-care instructions patients can find again
  • Care-plan and program tracking between visits

  • App design in your brand: your name, your colors, your icon on the home screen
  • Build for iPhone and Android from one codebase

A good fit — and a poor one

A good fit is a business with a repeat client relationship — a treatment series, a boarding regular, a training roster, a recall list — and one or two client tasks that happen over and over: booking, check-ins, reminders, photos, tracking.

A poor fit is a one-off transaction business, or an idea that needs a marketplace, payments network, or social platform behind it. We will say so in the first reply rather than scope the wrong build.

Support and limitations

  • Your store accounts, your enrollment. The app is published under your own Apple Developer and Google Play accounts. Enrolling — identity verification and the stores' own fees — is yours to complete; we cannot hold your store presence in our name and still call it yours.
  • Health information. The build does not store protected health information (PHI) on our side. If your app handles PHI, the features, the hosting, and the agreements behind it are your practice's compliance decisions under HIPAA — made with your compliance advisor, not from our say-so. This page is not legal advice, and we do not certify any build as HIPAA-compliant.
  • Not a medical device. A client app handles scheduling, forms, reminders, and tracking. It does not diagnose, treat, or monitor patients, and it is not a substitute for your clinical systems or your electronic health record.
  • Timelines are scoped, not promised loose. Your written scope carries the schedule, including the store review windows — Apple's and Google's review times are theirs, not ours, and the scope says how resubmission rounds are handled.
  • Mobile app, web app, or both. A build can be a store app for phones, a browser-based web app, or both — your written scope names which. Browser-based apps and internal tools may not need store accounts at all.
  • Wellness is not medical advice. Program features like the guide in the demo support habits, check-ins, and organization. They are not medical advice, diagnosis, or clinical care, and the demo's guide is a scripted example, not a clinician.
  • Store approval is the stores' decision. We build to the published guidelines and handle the review rounds in scope; no one can guarantee a store's approval, and we will not pretend otherwise.

How pricing works

A fixed build fee, set in your written scope. Before any work begins, the scope names the features, the screens, the exclusions — and the one fixed fee for that scope, paid in milestones: on scope, on store submission, on handover. There is no hourly meter running. A change after scope is a new written scope, priced before it is built.

An optional care plan, if you want the studio on call: $49–$79/month. Bounded in the same writing: store listing upkeep, operating-system update compatibility checks, and small content changes — with named exclusions and a stated response window. New features are never inside the care plan; they are scoped separately. After handover, with or without a care plan, the app is yours to run.

Payment details are confirmed in your written scope.

What you'll need to launch

  • Your store accounts (scope-dependent). If your build is a store app: an Apple Developer Program account ($99/year, paid by you to Apple) and/or a Google Play developer account ($25 one-time, paid by you to Google). Browser-based web apps and internal tools may need neither.
  • Your domain and hosting (required for web apps). A domain name and hosting, in your name — typically a modest yearly cost paid to your registrar/host.
  • Optional services, only if your scope uses them. Push-notification, payment-processing, database, or authentication services; each is named in the scope with its cost before work begins. Payment processors charge their own per-transaction fees.
  • Your enrollment steps. Store enrollment includes identity verification only you can complete — we walk you through it, and the accounts stay yours.

Ownership, handover, and support

  • Yours: the app, its store listings, your developer accounts, your domain, and your business data. The build happens in accounts established in your name wherever practical, so you hold the keys from day one.
  • Handover includes: the published app, documentation of how it is set up and how to make routine changes, and the account list — everything another developer would need to pick it up.
  • Scope and changes: the written scope names the included features. A request beyond it is a new written scope, priced before it is built — never a surprise line item.
  • Support. If something we delivered doesn't do what the written scope says, we fix it — that's a defect, not a new request. Anything beyond the scope is quoted in writing before work begins.
  • If we part ways: the app keeps running on your accounts. Any care plan simply ends; the third-party accounts and their fees remain yours, as they were from the start.
  • Licensed components: stock libraries, fonts, and platform services stay under their own licenses — we promise ownership only of what is ours to give.

Common questions

Who owns the app?

You do. The developer accounts, the store listings, the code, and the documentation are in your name and handed over to you. That is the same ownership rule as everything we build — if we ever part ways, the app and its listings stay right where they are: yours.

Why do I need my own Apple and Google accounts?

Because the stores publish apps under the account holder's name and control. Your patients and clients should find your app under your business, and you should be able to change or revoke access whenever you like. Apple and Google charge their own enrollment fees, paid by you directly to them.

Does the app handle patient health information?

Not on our side. The build does not store protected health information on Mobley Group or Mobley Labs systems. If the app you want would collect or store PHI, that shapes the design, the hosting, and the agreements — and those are your practice's compliance decisions to make with your own advisor. We flag it plainly at scope time rather than discover it mid-build.

How long does a build take?

Your written scope carries the timeline, broken into design, build, and store review. Store review windows belong to Apple and Google, so the scope states the plan for them — including how fix-and-resubmit rounds are handled — instead of a single date nobody controls.

Is this a medical device or a replacement for our practice software?

No. A client app covers the client-facing layer — forms, reminders, check-ins, instructions, tracking. It does not diagnose or treat, and it sits alongside your clinical and practice-management systems rather than replacing them.

What do you not do?

We do not build marketplaces, social apps, or apps whose core is collecting patient records. We do not hold your store accounts, your data, or your clients' information on our side. And we do not promise store approval or download numbers — anyone who does is selling you the promise, not the app.
