The call gets answered. Even when you can't.

An AI voice receptionist answers your business's calls in your name, answers the questions you've approved, captures appointment requests, and routes them to your team — where a person confirms every booking. Set up in your accounts, scoped in writing, handed over with documentation.

Who it's for — and the problem it solves

Who it's for: small businesses that miss calls they can't afford to miss — practices, studios, salons, and service shops where the team is with a client when the phone rings.

The problem: every unanswered call is a customer deciding whether to try the next name on the list. Voicemail catches some; the rest just move on. A voice receptionist answers every call in your name, handles the routine questions from your approved list, and routes the rest to your team.

What a setup includes

  • A written scope: the call flows, the questions it answers, and what it never handles — agreed before setup
  • A greeting and voice in your business's name, from your approved wording
  • An approved answer list: hours, location, services, prices you choose to state
  • Appointment-request capture: name, need, preferred time — routed to your team
  • Routing rules: what's routine, what's urgent, and when a call goes straight to a person
  • A summary to you after each call, and handover documentation for the whole setup

Support and limitations

  • A person confirms every booking. The receptionist captures and routes requests; your team approves them. Nothing is promised to a caller on your behalf beyond your approved wording.
  • Not for emergencies. The scope directs urgent callers to your stated protocol — for a practice, that includes telling emergency callers to hang up and dial 911.
  • It answers from your list. Anything outside the approved answers is captured and routed to you, not improvised.
  • It runs on provider services. The receptionist depends on a phone/voice provider and voice AI services; those platforms' availability and behavior are theirs. Your scope names the providers used.
  • Recording and consent. Call-recording and consent rules vary by state. Your scope states whether calls are recorded and what callers are told, and the setup follows it.
  • Not a staff replacement. It covers the calls nobody can reach — after hours, mid-appointment, all-lines-busy — and hands the rest back to your people.

What you'll need to launch

  • A business phone setup (required). Your existing number, or a new one we help you set up — held in your name, on your provider account.
  • Provider accounts (required). The phone/voice platform and voice AI service run under your accounts; their subscriptions are paid by you directly to the providers.
  • Usage charges (scope-dependent). Voice minutes and AI usage are billed by the providers at their rates, based on your call volume. Your written scope estimates them before launch; we don't set or mark up those rates.
  • Your approved answer list (required). The hours, services, and prices the receptionist may state — prepared with you before the first live call.

“No surprise platform costs. Before work begins, we'll explain any required third-party accounts, subscriptions, usage charges, or publishing fees. Where practical, these accounts are established in your name so you retain control.”

Pricing and ongoing costs

The setup is priced by written scope — your call types, your routing rules, your integrations. There is no shelf tier.

Ongoing costs are the providers', not ours: the phone/voice platform subscription and per-minute / AI usage charges, paid by you to the providers. Optional ongoing support from us is quoted separately, in writing, only if you want it.

Ownership, handover, and support

  • Yours: the number, the provider accounts, the configuration, and the call summaries. The setup is built under your accounts so the asset is yours.
  • Handover includes: documentation of what the receptionist answers, what it routes, and how to change any of it.
  • Scope and changes: new call types or new integrations are new written scopes, priced before they are built.
  • Support. If something we delivered doesn't do what the written scope says, we fix it — that's a defect, not a new request. Anything beyond the scope is quoted in writing before work begins.
  • If we part ways: the receptionist keeps running on your accounts and provider subscriptions; only our optional support ends.
